What is reverse recruiting and how does it work?

In normal recruiting, an employer pays a recruiter to find people. In reverse recruiting, you hire a team to find the jobs and reach employers for you.

By the Virtue Careers team. Updated November 6, 2026. 3 minute read.

Most people know how normal recruiting works: a company hires a recruiter to find people for a job. Reverse recruiting flips that. You hire a team to find the jobs and reach the employers on your behalf.

The short answer

Reverse recruiting is a service where a team does the hands-on work of your job search for you. They help you decide which roles to target, search for openings, send applications and contact hiring managers, recruiters and useful people in your network. You focus on interviews and on the decisions that only you can make.

What the team does

  • Clarifies your target: the roles, industries, company types and locations that fit you.
  • Prepares your documents: your resume, LinkedIn profile and cover letters, written to match the target.
  • Finds opportunities: job boards, company websites and roles that are not widely advertised.
  • Reaches out: applies and contacts hiring managers and recruiters directly with a clear, short message.
  • Tracks responses: keeps a record of every application and follow up so nothing is dropped.
  • Prepares you for interviews: practice, stories and questions to ask.

How it differs from a recruiter or a coach

  • A recruiter or search firm is paid by the employer, works for the employer and is trying to fill one specific role. They may not contact you unless you fit that role.
  • A career coach gives advice and accountability. You usually still do the searching and outreach yourself.
  • Reverse recruiting is paid for by you, works for you and reaches out to many employers in your target area.

Who it suits

  • Busy executives who do not have time to run a search properly.
  • People who need a confidential search while still employed.
  • Career changers who need help explaining how their experience fits a new field.
  • Professionals who are applying but not getting replies.
  • Leaders returning to work after a gap or a move to a new country.

What to expect

Expect the first stage to be about positioning: a conversation about your goals, then your resume and LinkedIn profile. Outreach and applications follow. Interviews depend on the market and on how well your background fits the roles you are targeting.

No honest provider can promise you a job or a start date. Senior searches commonly take several months, and the result depends on the market, your experience and your flexibility. What a good service can do is make sure you are applying with strong documents, reaching the right people and not wasting weeks on things that do not work.

Questions to ask any provider

  1. Who will actually do the outreach, and can I see what is sent?
  2. How do you protect confidentiality if I am currently employed?
  3. How will you report progress, and how often?
  4. What exactly is included in the price, and what is extra?
  5. Do you make any guarantees? Be careful with big promises about jobs or salaries.
  6. Can I see examples of the documents you have produced?

What it costs

Prices vary widely between providers, so compare what is included rather than only the total. At Virtue Careers, job search help starts at $875 at manager level and rises with seniority, and every package begins with a free review. You can see all packages on our pricing page.

Can you do it yourself?

Yes. The core steps are the same whether you use a team or not:

  1. Decide on two or three specific target roles.
  2. Update your resume and LinkedIn profile to match.
  3. Make a list of 30 to 50 target companies.
  4. Contact people at those companies, not just the job portal.
  5. Keep a simple tracker of every application and follow up.

The real difference is time and consistency. A team keeps the process moving every week, even when your calendar is full.
