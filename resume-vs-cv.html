Resume vs CV: what to use in the US, UK, Canada and Nigeria

Same document, different names and rules. Here is what employers in each country usually expect.

By the Virtue Careers team. Updated November 6, 2026. 3 minute read.

In this guide
  1. The short answer
  2. Quick comparison by country
  3. When a CV means something longer
  4. Applying in another country
  5. If you are an executive
  6. Simple rules to follow

Resume or CV? The answer depends on where you are applying and what kind of job it is. In many countries the two words mean the same thing. In others they are different documents. This guide explains the difference and what employers usually expect in the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada and Nigeria.

The short answer

  • United States and Canada: for most jobs you send a resume. A CV is a longer document used mainly for academic, research and some medical roles.
  • United Kingdom, Ireland, Nigeria and many other countries: CV is the normal word for the document you send for most jobs. It is usually about two pages, so in practice it works like a resume.
  • Always follow the instructions in the job posting. If it says "send your CV", send your CV, whichever country you are in.

In the US, UK and Canada, employers generally prefer not to receive photos or details like age and marital status, because they want to avoid decisions that could be seen as unfair. That is why those details are left off.

When a CV means something longer

In the US and Canada, a CV (short for curriculum vitae) is a complete record of your academic and professional life. It can run many pages and lists items such as research, publications, grants, teaching, conference talks and awards. You send it for roles in universities, research institutions and some medical and scientific positions. For nearly everything else in those two countries, a short resume is the right choice.

Applying in another country

If you are applying across borders, a few small changes make you look prepared:

  • Spelling: match the country, for example "organization" in the US and "organisation" in the UK and Nigeria.
  • Paper size: US Letter for the US and Canada, A4 for the UK, Nigeria and most other countries.
  • Dates: write the month as a word, for example "March 2024". This avoids confusion between day and month order.
  • Phone numbers: include the country code, such as +1, +44 or +234.
  • Explain local employers: add a short phrase so a foreign reader understands the size of the company, for example "a leading telecom provider with 40 million subscribers".
  • Work authorization: some employers care about it. Mention it only if it is true and relevant.

If you are an executive

The advice is the same: show the scope of what you have led and the results you created. International experience is a strength, so state it clearly, for example "led a team across five countries". Our executive resume guide covers the full structure.

Simple rules to follow

  1. Follow the wording and file instructions in the job posting.
  2. If it is an academic or research role, use a full CV.
  3. For other roles in the US and Canada, use a one to two page resume.
  4. For other roles in the UK and Nigeria, use a two page CV.
  5. Match the spelling, paper size and date style to the country of the employer.
